Feed

Posts by the people the current account follows, newest first. Each one is a signed event a relay handed us, stored verbatim as events/<id>.json and never changed; feed.json is just the order.

People

On nostr a person is a public key: 64 hex characters, or the same number written as npub…. There is no directory; you know someone by their key. Each account has its own list, its follows.json; this is the current account's. The relay clients ask for every account's follows at once, and the feed shows the current one's. What we know about each person is their own profile event (kind 0), kept as profiles/<pubkey>.json and replaced whenever a relay sends a newer one.

Relays

Relays are the ship's, not an account's: one socket per relay serves every account here, subscribed to all their follows at once. A relay is a plain websocket server anyone can run. It stores the events people send it and answers subscriptions: a client opens a socket, sends ["REQ", …filter], and the relay streams every matching event it has, says EOSE (end of stored events), then keeps the socket open and pushes new ones as they arrive. There is no login and no single relay; the same post usually arrives from several, and is stored once, by id. Each client here is one fiber holding one socket. live means the socket is open and the stored history is in; a client that loses its socket reconnects on its own, waiting a little longer after each failure. Each writes its state to relays/<host>.json.

Posts

A post is a kind-1 event: the current account's key, a timestamp, the text, a signature. It is written to outbox/<id>.json first, then pushed down every open relay socket; each relay answers OK (accepted or not) and that verdict lands in the same file. Publishing needs a current account (Accounts tab).

Accounts

An account is one keypair, kept as accounts/<pubkey>/. This ship holds any number; the current one (me.json) is what every action signs as, and what the feed, people and posts are of. Each one opens into its keys and the profile it publishes.
